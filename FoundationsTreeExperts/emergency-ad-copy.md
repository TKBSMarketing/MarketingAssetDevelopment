# Foundations Tree Experts — 24/7 Emergency Ad Set

One pool of creative, two uses:

- **`[ALWAYS-ON]`** — runs continuously alongside the lead gen campaign. Catches the person at 11pm with a limb on the garage, the one whose neighbor's tree just came down, the one who *just* realized the leaning oak moved. There is no storm required for a tree emergency.
- **`[STORM]`** — parked in the dormant Storm Response campaign. Flip on within hours of a severe weather event, run 5–7 days, flip off.

Every ad below is tagged. Nothing here needs to be rewritten between the two modes — you just turn ads on and off.

**Phone:** 1-833-TREE-365 · **Site:** foundationstreeexperts.com
**Claims used (all pre-approved, already live in existing copy):** 26 years · ISA Certified Arborist on staff · licensed · fully insured · cleanup included · real person answers 24/7 · free estimates

---

## Copy length — deliberate split

The house default is longform (4–6 sentences), because it outperforms for considered purchases. Emergency is the one place that breaks:

| Mode | Length | Why |
|------|--------|-----|
| `[ALWAYS-ON]` | Longform (4–6 sentences) | Reader is *browsing*, not bleeding. They have time. Longform builds the trust that gets them to save the number before they need it. |
| `[STORM]` | Short (2–4 lines) | Reader has a tree on their house right now. They are scanning on a phone, possibly without power. Every extra sentence is a chance to bounce. |

Both are below. Don't mix them up — running longform during a storm burst is the fastest way to waste the budget spike.

# ACTIVATION PLAYBOOK

## Always-on setup
- Add E1–E4 to the existing Lead Gen campaign as a **separate ad set** ("FTE – 24/7 Emergency – Ann Arbor"), or run them as a low-budget third campaign at ~$2–3/day.
- **CTA button: Call Now**, not Get Quote. Emergency intent converts on the phone, not a form. This is the one place where sending them to the site adds friction.
- Keep the website destination in the ad as a secondary path, but the phone number is on every creative for a reason.
- Expect a *lower* CTR than the lead gen ads and a *much* higher call-to-job rate. Judge these on calls, not clicks.

## Storm activation — within hours
1. Turn on **Campaign 3: Storm Response** with S1–S3.
2. Bump daily budget to **$25–30/day**, pulled from Campaign 1 (pause the lowest-CPL lead gen ad for the week).
3. Tighten radius to the actual hit area if the storm was localized — a 10-mile Ann Arbor circle wastes money if only the west side got hit.
4. Turn off the `[ALWAYS-ON]` emergency ads for the burst window so they don't compete in the same auction.
5. **Day 5–7: turn it all off**, restore Campaign 1 budget, re-enable always-on. Storm intent decays fast; spend past day 7 is dead money.

## What to watch
| Metric | Read |
|--------|------|
| Calls (not clicks) | The only real success metric for this set |
| CPM during storm week | Will spike — everyone's bidding. Accept it; intent is worth it |
| Frequency | Above 4 in a 7-day burst means the radius is too tight |
| CTR | Lower than lead gen is normal and fine here |

## Claim discipline
Everything in this file uses claims already approved and live. Two things are **not** claimed anywhere, because they haven't been confirmed with David — do not add them without asking:
- Insurance claim documentation / working directly with adjusters
- Any specific response-time guarantee ("on site in 60 minutes," etc.)

Both are strong emergency angles if true. Worth a two-minute conversation with David before the next round.
